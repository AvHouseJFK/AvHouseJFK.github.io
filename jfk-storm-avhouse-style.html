Severe Thunderstorms Sweep Through JFK, Widebodies Forced Into Holding

Published August 7, 2026

Severe thunderstorms swept through John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) on August 7, 2026, causing major disruptions to flights across the airport. During the storm, aircraft were forced into holding patterns around JFK, with widebody aircraft including Boeing 777s, Airbus A380s, and other flights circling as severe weather moved through the area.

At one point, JFK was experiencing severe disruptions, with hundreds of flights delayed or cancelled as thunderstorms, heavy rain, and dangerous weather impacted operations.

Aircraft in Holding

As the storm cell tracked through the approach corridor, JFK approach control stacked arriving traffic in holding patterns to keep aircraft clear of the worst of the weather. Widebody types were among those seen circling, alongside a mix of narrowbody flights waiting for gaps in the cell to resume approaches.

Operational Notes

Thunderstorm activity over the New York area forced controllers to slow the arrival flow into JFK, pushing inbound traffic into extended holding while the worst of the cell passed. As delays built on the ground and in the air, the ripple effect pushed cancellation and delay figures well above a typical operating day, with roughly one in five flights cancelled outright and over a third of all traffic delayed.

Conditions gradually improved as the storm moved off, allowing approach and departure flows to normalize through the evening, though residual delays continued to work through the schedule.

Conclusion

August 7, 2026, marked one of JFK's most disruptive thunderstorm events of the summer, with severe weather grounding hundreds of flights and stacking widebody and narrowbody traffic alike into holding patterns around the airport. For spotters and passengers alike, it was a stark reminder of how quickly summer convective weather can reshape operations at one of the world's busiest airports.
